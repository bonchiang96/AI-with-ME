# Book Development Workflow

## 1. Use

1. The user selects this Workflow when a Task starts. The Agent may propose it.
2. Approved Scope selects its application.
3. Governing working arrangements may define outputs, locations and assigned Agents.
4. Each Identity runs its own steps under its Role Lifecycle.

Develop the user's fragmented or conversational input while preserving core thinking and intended meaning.
Contribute new ideas only when explicitly requested.

## 2. Stages and owners

Flow: Foundation and Outline → Book Knowledge Base → rough draft → user review → revisions → Content Edit → Copy Edit → finished manuscript.

1. The Book Meeting Agent develops the Book Foundation and Outline with the user.
2. It saves confirmed outcomes to the Book Knowledge Base.
3. The shared Execution Agent drafts the manuscript with complete QA.
4. The user reviews the draft in a Meeting.
5. Apply revisions under the user review and revision rule in [meeting-agent](../meeting-agent/SKILL.md). Continue until the rough manuscript is complete.
6. Complete Content Edit and Copy Edit. Apply the same revision rule to both edits.

Earlier stages may be revisited when new information materially changes the Book.
Change the active Book only through explicit user direction.
The default is one full draft. A large Book may use Chapter-by-Chapter drafting.

## 3. Foundation

1. **Purpose.** Before manuscript development, clarify WHY the user wants to write the book and WHY NOW it matters.
   Use this purpose when choices compete, confidence falls or direction is lost.
2. **Book Idea.** Establish one main Book Idea and singular focus.
   - If it is unclear, explore expertise, paid work, deep interests, recurring requests for advice and repeated questions or conversations.
   - When ideas compete, consider fastest completion with existing material, likelihood of finishing, motivation and satisfaction.
3. **Ideal Reader and 4Ps.** Clarify four decisions:
   1. **Person.** The specific reader to help, entertain or reach.
   2. **Pain.** A problem, need, tension or desire the reader already recognizes.
   3. **Promise.** The outcome, benefit, experience or value the reader should receive.
   4. **Price.** The intended price or pricing position for the genre and format.
4. Use Person as the primary writing reference.
   Align voice, examples, explanations, stories and detail with that reader.
5. Fiction and children's books may address the user or readers seeking their intended genre or experience.

## 4. Architecture

1. Maintain one living Book Architecture: a Mind Map for discovery and an Outline for structure.
2. Exhaust relevant material before perfecting structure.
3. Capture ideas, stories, experiences, examples, evidence, lessons, repeated explanations and reference-triggered ideas.
4. Early material may be messy, repetitive, incomplete or unordered. Organize it progressively.
5. Sequence Parts, Chapters and supporting points for natural reader progression.
6. Do not delay drafting to perfect the first architecture.

**Nonfiction.** Identify major themes or Parts, usually three to seven broad Sections where useful.
Organize Chapters and supporting points beneath them.
Give an idea its own Chapter only when its meaning justifies one.

**Fiction.** Where applicable, establish this progression:

1. **Set-Up.** The hero, motivation, setting, genre, world rules, major conflicts and important tropes.
2. **Inciting Incident.** An event that changes the hero's path and cannot simply be ignored.
3. **First Slap.** A stage that escalates the stakes severely.
4. **Second Slap with Hope.** A stage that escalates events further while revealing possible progress or victory.
5. **Climax.** Connect it to the Inciting Incident. Resolve the central conflict appropriately.

## 5. Optional Chapter loop

When the Identity customizes a large Book for Chapter-by-Chapter drafting, use:

1. Content Extraction.
2. Chapter Structure.
3. Confirmation.
4. Chapter Draft.

Apply these rules in the loop:

1. Use focused questions to elicit the user's thinking, experiences, stories, examples, reasoning and intended effect.
2. Do not replace missing core ideas without explicit permission to contribute.
3. Before drafting, identify Chapter purpose, the key message, supporting points, stories or examples and sequence.
4. Confirm meaning and structure with the user before drafting from them.
5. Repeat the loop until the rough manuscript is complete.

## 6. Drafting

1. Draft confirmed material point by point for the Ideal Reader.
2. Develop author voice from the user's communication and confirmed direction.
3. Accept spoken or conversational input. Organize it into coherent prose.
4. Complete the book's meaning before polishing language.
5. Capture improvements for later. Do not repeatedly edit unfinished Chapters.
6. Maintain a practical completion plan with Chapter or time checkpoints where useful.
7. Unless explicitly included, exclude title, cover, marketing, launch and publishing work from rough drafting.
8. Preserve blocked gaps or placeholders and continue where possible.

When momentum falls:

1. Revisit WHY, WHY NOW, the active stage and the next concrete step.
2. Review or adjust completion goals under user direction.
3. Restore a sustainable writing commitment.
4. Offer accountability support when useful.

An imperfect complete rough draft satisfies the completion priority better than isolated polished Chapters.

## 7. Author Review

1. After rough completion, perform at least one complete review from the Ideal Reader's perspective.
2. Prefer verbal review when practical.
3. Identify errors, gaps, insufficient stories or supporting material, unclear meaning and departures from author voice or intent.
4. Treat discomfort with the first complete draft as part of the creative process.
5. When direction is lost, revisit Purpose, Ideal Reader and Promise.
6. Progress when sufficiently ready for Content Edit.

## 8. Content Edit

1. Resolve content gaps, tangents, duplication, flow, transitions, supporting material, sequence and unclear meaning.
2. Assess fulfillment of the Promise for the Ideal Reader.
3. When justified, move, merge, expand, remove or restructure material.
4. Resolve meaning, content and structure before sentence polishing.

## 9. Copy Edit

1. Begin when content and structure are sufficiently settled.
2. Improve spelling, grammar, punctuation, redundancy, wording, clarity, flow, consistency and readability.
3. Preserve the confirmed meaning, voice and Promise.

## 10. Finished manuscript

1. Require content-level and copy-level review.
2. The book is finished when it is clear, coherent, readable, consistent and fit for the Ideal Reader.
3. Apply a practical completion boundary rather than endless polishing.

## 11. Book State

Keep Book progress in the owning Identity Context:

- The active stage.
- The current Part or Chapter.
- Open content gaps.
- The next step.

Keep confirmed Book decisions in the Book Knowledge Base.
Transfer stage ownership only through a normal Handoff.

## 12. Deliverables and Language Editions

1. Book Minutes and Job Records follow the standard Meeting and Job records.
2. The manuscript is one Deliverable containing its Chapters.
3. One Book cycle produces one finished manuscript in the Base Edition language.
4. Complete and publish the Base Edition before an additional-language cycle.
5. Each additional language is a separate cycle.
6. Redraft each Language Edition from the final Base Edition manuscript and the Book Knowledge Base.
7. A Language Edition is a redraft, not a translation.
